# 3D War-Room Operating Model

## Purpose

The war-room is a **protected expert design space** for developing the Business Context Framework quickly and coherently.

Its purpose is not to represent every stakeholder or achieve organisation-wide consensus.

Its purpose is to bring enough expertise together to define a strong point of view that can be tested.

## Why this approach

Discover + Define has already exposed the systemic problems.

Continuing to broaden consultation before defining a model risks reproducing the same assumptions, system boundaries and operating logic that created the fragmentation.

The war-room deliberately creates some distance from those inherited constraints.

> **We know enough to propose what good looks like. Evidence from delivery will tell us where we are wrong.**

## Suggested expertise

Keep the core group small.

Bring together people who can contribute different forms of judgement, for example:

- customer experience / service design;
- frontline or operational service expertise;
- customer accounts / CRM;
- service information / knowledge;
- data / analytics;
- digital experience;
- architecture or integration expertise when needed.

Participation is based on the problem being solved, not organisational representation.

## Working rules

### 1. Principle-led, not consensus-led

The group should seek coherence, not unanimity.

### 2. Current state is evidence, not the blueprint

Current processes and systems help explain constraints and available assets. They do not determine the future model.

### 3. Challenge inherited boundaries

Ask what context the interaction needs before asking which system currently owns it.

### 4. Make assumptions visible

Where evidence is incomplete, record the assumption and move forward.

### 5. Design for testing

Every meaningful framework decision should eventually create or sharpen a testable experience hypothesis.

### 6. Evidence has veto power

A strong design opinion can start the test. Real-world evidence can overturn it.

## Working cadence

A practical cycle:

```text
FRAME
What do we believe good needs?

        ↓

MODEL
Add / refine concepts and relationships

        ↓

MAP
Can today's sources fit into it?

        ↓

OPPORTUNITY
What better experience becomes possible?

        ↓

HAND TO 4D
Turn it into a test
```

## Output discipline

The war-room should produce only:

- framework changes;
- explicit assumptions;
- decisions;
- candidate experience opportunities;
- questions that genuinely block testing.

Avoid producing large consultation packs or exhaustive future-state documentation before evidence requires it.
